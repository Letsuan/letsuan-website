12.03 Meters, Eight Worlds

The container's interior measures 12.03m long by 2.35m wide, a floor area of roughly 28.27 square meters, about 8.5 ping. Fitting eight full exhibit units and a complete circulation path into that footprint meant every dimension and layout decision had to be calculated with precision, with no inch to waste.

Since 2001, the National Museum of History has operated Taiwan's first permanent mobile museum vehicle, converting a 40-foot shipping container into a traveling exhibition platform that carries the museum's collection to schools and communities across the island.

In 2017, the museum re-fitted its existing container vehicle to launch "Kool Beasts Are Coming: A Fantastical Journey Between the Real and the Imagined," built around national treasures and important artifacts from the collection: a zoomorphic vessel stand, a green-glazed pottery ox, and a tri-color glazed horse, combined with imagery of the Four Guardian Beasts and the twelve zodiac animals. Digital interactivity and traditional cultural education were packed into a single space that can drive itself to you.

The Vehicle Came First

The existing container vehicle was towed first to a flatbed yard in Chiayi for structural repair, an MEP system upgrade, and an air-conditioning replacement. Only once the base vehicle was in working order was it transported to a woodworking facility in Houli for interior fabrication. Display props and sculptural elements were CNC-machined, with metalwork completed on site to finish the assembly and installation of the full display system. This wasn't interior-design logic so much as fixing a vehicle to a usable standard before exhibition design got to have an opinion.

Designing Around a Door That Still Has to Close

At the far end of the circulation, a curved partition wall defines a self-contained AR interactive game zone, its surface extending the mythical-beast illustration motif, with color-blocked zones guiding visitors through each unit in sequence. The container still had to function as a container: it needed to open, close, and be transported, so exhibition design couldn't follow the logic of a fixed venue.

The placement of every display case, multimedia device, light fixture, and electrical system had to work within the swing range of the container doors and the space constraints of the vehicle in its packed-down state, delivering a complete display experience when open while making sure nothing collided or blocked the doors from closing when it was time to move.

Eight Units, Eight Solutions

All eight exhibit units were, in the end, solving the same problem: how to fit that many different kinds of interaction into 28.27 square meters without any of them crowding each other out.

The cleverest solution belongs to "Kool Beasts Revealed: The Magic of 3D Printing," which displays 3D-printed replicas of the zoomorphic vessel stand, the tri-color horse, and the green-glazed ox alongside interpretive text, audio guides, and a video on the production process. Because the container space is so tight, the audio guide was designed around the image of a paper-cup phone: visitors have to lean in close to the device to hear it, which keeps sound from leaking out and disturbing others while playing on the nostalgic charm of early communication devices, turning a spatial constraint into an interactive idea.

The other seven zones each found their own solution. "Animals, Real and Imagined" links tiger, turtle, and deer with mythical creatures like the qilin, phoenix, and dragon through different collection materials. A kiosk learning zone pairs picture quizzes with puzzles. "Puzzle Zoo" uses tangram pieces to build animal shapes. "Words in the Making" lets visitors experience how pictographs evolved into written characters. "Mirror, Mirror, Coolest Beast" uses AR to trigger animation. "I'm the Star" offers three motion-sensing games. "Rubbings to Take Home" lets visitors collect stamped rubbings of the Four Guardian Beasts. Whatever solved each unit's puzzle still had to fit inside a container that opens, closes, and drives away.
