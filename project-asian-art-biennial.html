The Lawn Needed More Protection Than the Artwork Did 2013 Asian Art Biennial

CLIENT

National Taiwan Museum of Fine Arts

LOCATION

Taichung, Taiwan

YEAR

2013-2014

CATEGORY

Construction

Ten Light Boxes, One Lawn That Couldn't Be Damaged

In 2013, the artist collective LuxuryLogico took part in the National Taiwan Museum of Fine Arts' "2013 Asian Art Biennial: Everyday Life" with a piece called I LOVE TAIWAN, using a large array of light-box installations to turn a well-worn Taiwanese catchphrase into a variable-scale outdoor artwork scattered across the museum's lawn. We handled the on-site construction and fabrication of the piece.

The Lawn Was Part of the Work

The site was the museum's expansive outdoor lawn, which functioned as both backdrop and part of the artwork's effect: light boxes standing scattered across the green, lighting up at night into a landscape of light. There was one non-negotiable requirement for the build: fix and wire all ten light boxes without damaging the lawn itself. That ruled out the heavy machinery normally used for outdoor construction, large-scale foundation excavation, and anything that would leave exposed soil or compaction marks behind, since the lawn's condition was tied directly to how the artwork looked and to the museum's ongoing maintenance obligations.

Building Without a Trace

For each light box's post and base, we skipped pouring concrete altogether and designed a custom base component to carry the load instead, using a low-disturbance fixing method that avoided large-scale digging. Power cabling between the ten light boxes ran along existing hard-paved surfaces wherever possible, or was buried shallow underground, cutting down how often equipment had to be dragged across the grass.

We used manual labor and small tools instead of heavy machinery, working zone by zone in batches so the foot traffic and load on any one section of lawn stayed within what it could recover from. Large canvas sheets covered the main routes and work areas, so all foot traffic, tool storage, and material handling sat on canvas rather than directly on the grass.

Once each installation was positioned, we checked the lawn zone by zone, backfilling and leveling any compression marks or loosened soil so the surface was flat again before opening day. With teardown in mind, the light-box structures and materials were chosen for roughly 90% recyclability, so most of the material could be reused or recycled once the show closed rather than becoming waste.

Result

The ten-light-box I LOVE TAIWAN installation went up across the museum's outdoor lawn without incident, hidden among the greenery by day and lighting up into a sea of text by night. The lawn came through the install exactly as it went in.
