Exhibition Design, Construction

30 Meters of Fossil, 6 Meters of Wall

From Dragon to Beast was the largest, highest-specification international exhibition the National Museum of Natural Science had staged to date. Over a hundred rare fossils crossed the sea from the Gansu Geological Museum and the Hezheng Paleozoological Museum, including the largest and most completely preserved sauropod dinosaur ever discovered in Asia, the 30-meter-long Yongjing Titanosaur, alongside more than 140 significant pieces from the museum's own collection, many displayed outside mainland China for the first time. We took on the full design-build contract: display structures, custom cases, lighting and visual integration, flooring, and outdoor weatherproofing, against international-grade preservation standards, a tight schedule, and a highly complex mix of cross-disciplinary trades.

A Wall That Had to Stand on Its Own

The Dragon Hall was the centerpiece of the exhibition, an independent display wall standing 6 meters tall that had to carry the enormous suspended load of the giant dinosaur fossil and its heavy steel armature entirely on its own, without leaning on or damaging the existing building structure. Precise structural center-of-gravity calculations and load-distribution analysis led us to a self-supporting freestanding structural method: the wall itself became a complete structural system, holding up a 30-meter dinosaur without ever leaning on a single beam or column of the museum.

Sealed Once, No Second Chance

Under international museum preservation standards, once the fossils were positioned, the display case glass had to be sealed in a single pass, and from that moment until the end of the exhibition it couldn't be opened again. That meant every light fixture's angle, every color temperature, the microclimate humidity control inside the case, and the flatness of every finish surface had to be exactly right before the seal went on. There was no adjusting afterward; every parameter had to be the final answer on the first attempt.

Color as Geology

More than seven dedicated color schemes and lighting systems, one per display case, used color and optics as a metaphor for different geological eras, clearly separating fossil groups and evolutionary stages so visitors could sense they were walking through a journey spanning hundreds of millions of years just from the shifts in light and tone, without reading a single wall panel in full.

The floor got the same level of calculation: a high-strength raised timber flooring system with hidden LED light strips seamlessly embedded within it doubled as ambient exhibition lighting and safe wayfinding for large crowds, and the extended outdoor exhibition zone received additional high-standard moisture-proofing, UV resistance, and weatherproof coating to keep the wood flooring and display elements from warping or fading under prolonged sun and rain.

A Collapse Near the Finish Line

With installation nearly complete, an existing staircase, sealed off years earlier and unused for a long time, suddenly became structurally unstable, causing a localized floor collapse in the exhibition hall. We completed a safety-focused repair first, laying full carpet coverage over the area to keep the opening on schedule and circulation safe, and left the permanent structural repair for the museum to carry out through its standard procedures after the exhibition closed. The project was delivered with zero safety incidents and zero schedule delays, and the 30-meter dinosaur arrived in its hall exactly on time.
